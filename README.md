# liquid-bl-rochpl

**Current focus: [`gpu/`](gpu/).** Calibrate any liquid-cooled GPU from its power and junction sensor, then predict its junction temperature from power alone. Start with `gpu/README.md` and `gpu/gpu_validation.ipynb`. A working copy of the cabinet and cooling-group work is in [`cabinet/`](cabinet/).

Applies the 1D liquid boundary-layer model (`reference/liquid boundary layer under square wave heating.pdf`, eq. 32)
to a Frontier rocHPL power trace loaded with Ampere.

- `liquid_bl_rochpl.ipynb`: the analysis (run with the `ampere_dev` conda env).
  1. Replicates `reference/liquid_BL_results.pdf` (20 W/cm², P = 50 ms, t_on = 25 ms).
  2. Loads `data/rochpl-frontier` with Ampere and converts GCD energy over the full trace (82 s) into wall heat flux.
  3. Uses the FFT to choose square waves and fits a sum of them to the flux, window by window (125 ms windows,
     12 gated square waves each, ~7 900 in total), so the ms-scale spikes and dips are kept (flux R² ≈ 0.99).
  4. Applies eq. 32 to each square wave and superposes the results to get the liquid superheat field over the full trace
     (evaluated by FFT convolution), by period band, and checked against the model driven directly by the trace.
  5. Catalogues the square waves (periodic wall swing and penetration depth).
- `make_notebook.py`: regenerates the notebook source.
- `figures/`, `results/`: outputs written by the notebook.
- `data/rochpl-frontier/`: symlinks to `~/Documents/ampere/examples/hpl-trace` (rank 0 call graph + node metrics).

Assumptions to check: fluid properties (recovered from the results PDF, HFE-7100-like) and `A_HEATER_CM2` (14.5 cm²).

## Frontier cooling-group model

- `frontier_cooling_group.ipynb` (generated by `make_cooling_notebook.py`, same `ampere_dev` env): models a Frontier cooling group
  (3 cabinets + 1 CDU, `3Cab_POW.txt`) and the MI250X junction, and runs arbitrary power traces through the chain
  GPU power → node → cabinet AC → cabinet thermal mass → CDU heat exchanger + valve controller → coolant → junction temperature / heat flux.
  1. Cooling-group telemetry and energy balance.
  2. Node sum → cabinet AC: measured vs. the ExaDigiT RAPS loss model.
  3. Cabinet / CDU / controller fits (train t < 1400 s, test on the rest), closed-loop replay from AC power + facility supply only.
  4. 3-node Cauer junction network per MI250X package fitted to the rocHPL trace's junction sensor; heat flux at die exit and coolant wall.
  5. One node → 128-node cabinet (synchronised vs. desynchronised copies).
  6. `run_chain(node_sum_W, gpu_W, pit)` end to end: HPL replay, a periodic workload, a workload-period sweep.
- Outputs: `figures/cg_*.png`, `results/cooling_group_params.json`, `results/gpu_junction_params.csv`, `results/cooling_group_period_sweep.csv`.

Assumptions to check: column meanings in `3Cab_POW.txt` (PLF in gpm, RFNP = Redfish node sum), water as coolant for gpm conversions,
and the GPU coolant = cabinet supply + a per-package offset fitted on the single-node run.

## Worst workloads, chip lifetime and cooling decisions

- `frontier_workload_stress.ipynb` (generated by `make_workload_stress_notebook.py`, same env): scores a catalogue of workloads for chip and
  cooling stress, scans workload rhythms from 0.05 s to 2 h, estimates expansion and contraction, compares cooling and scheduling options,
  models one GPU on a cold plate with variable flow, and ends with `predict_cooling(cabinet_power_kW)`, one self-contained function.
- `frontier_model.py`: the fitted models as importable functions. It reads `results/cooling_group_params.json` and `results/gpu_junction_params.csv`,
  so run `frontier_cooling_group.ipynb` first. The `predict_cooling` constants are copied from those files when the notebook is generated.
- Outputs: `figures/stress_*.png`, `results/workload_stress_table.csv`, `results/workload_period_scan.csv`, `results/workload_expansion.csv`, `results/cooling_decisions_whatif.csv`.

Damage numbers are comparisons between workloads, not lifetimes. Assumptions to check: the fatigue exponents (from solder studies on other packages),
the valve limits (only the range seen in one run), and the flow exponent for the single-GPU model (assumed; flow past a GPU was never varied in the data).

## The cooling model in C, and fitting it to another machine

- `predict_cooling.c`: the cooling model as a C function that takes one power sample at a time, plus a command-line program. The constants are commented at the top.
- `fit_cooling_model.py`: estimates every constant from one CDU group's telemetry (format at the top of the script) and writes a constants file for the C code.
  It tests the result on held-out data and warns when the run can't support the numbers.

## Estimating the coolant from GPU data, in C

- `predict_gpu.c`: `predict_gpu_coolant(gpu, power_W, junction_C, timestamp_s)` returns the estimated coolant temperature at that GPU, from measured power and junction temperature.
- `fit_cooling_model.c`: the same parameter search as `fit_cooling_model.py` for the cooling group, plus the single-GPU fit, with no dependencies beyond libm.
- `frontier_rochpl_cooling.ipynb` (generated by `make_rochpl_cooling_notebook.py`): runs the rocHPL trace through `predict_gpu.c` end to end and graphs the estimated coolant. Its files go in `rochpl_gpu/`.
- `./reproduce_constants.sh`: regenerates `gpu_frontier.h` (read by `predict_gpu.c`) and `cooling_frontier.h` from the raw data, via `export_fit_data.py`.
